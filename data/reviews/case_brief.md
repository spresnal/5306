## Disputed issues and allegations

### 1. Seller misrepresentation and concealment

The buyers believe the sellers made materially inaccurate representations regarding the property's condition, history, square footage, and completed repairs. The principal allegations are:

**Bat infestation and remediation**

- The sellers represented that a $25,000 insurance claim funded comprehensive bat exclusion, removal of contaminated insulation and waste, disinfection, and reinsulation in September 2024.
- Derrick stated in writing before closing that the work had been completed and there had been no further issues.
- After possession, the buyers discovered bat guano, suspected entry points, and evidence of continued bat activity.
- The buyers report that a wildlife specialist found indications of incomplete remediation, including contaminated insulation apparently covered with newer insulation.
- Derrick subsequently identified Admiral Wildlife Services as the company he believed performed the work. The buyers report that an Admiral representative denied servicing the property.
- The buyers suspect the sellers overstated or falsely described the remediation performed. Whether the original work occurred, its scope, and whether the sellers knowingly misrepresented it remain unresolved.

**Water intrusion and drainage**

- The sellers disclosed two historical water-related insurance claims only after the buyers' insurance provider identified them.
- Derrick described the 2021 claim as an inquiry regarding an old bathroom stain and the 2023 claim as a precaution related to retaining-wall construction and patio drainage.
- He represented that the drainage issue was corrected and no additional problems had occurred.
- After closing, the buyers received accounts from neighbors indicating prior water problems and discovered conditions suggesting historical moisture intrusion.
- The buyers question whether the sellers minimized recurring water problems or withheld information about the extent and persistence of those conditions.
- The sellers' knowledge, the timing of any actual intrusion, and whether the relevant conditions were fully resolved have not been independently established.

**Square footage**

- The property was marketed at approximately 8,555 square feet.
- Before closing, the buyers specifically questioned conflicting square-footage figures and requested architectural plans.
- Darcy reported county assessor measurements rather than obtaining an independent measurement.
- The sellers reportedly deferred access to the architectural plans until possession.
- After closing, the buyers identified plans suggesting approximately 6,702 square feet and a historical listing reporting 7,383 finished square feet.
- The buyers believe the property contains substantially less finished living area than represented and that the discrepancy affected its purchase value.
- Whether the sellers possessed contradictory measurements or prior appraisals, knew the advertised figure was inaccurate, or deliberately withheld information is disputed.

**Other alleged undisclosed or misrepresented defects**

The buyers have additionally reported:

- Main-floor fireplace with cracked flue tiles and an allegedly undersized flue.
- Basement wood-burning fireplace with rust and evidence of overheating, reportedly requiring replacement.
- Plumbing leakage and evidence consistent with a longstanding water problem.
- HVAC equipment that failed shortly after possession.
- Defective or aging appliances marketed as high-end equipment.
- Pest activity, inadequate sealing, and other conditions not apparent during the limited inspection process.

The buyers contend these conditions may reflect inaccurate seller-disclosure answers or omissions. Their existence alone does not establish seller knowledge or legal liability. Professional reports, prior repair records, and the original signed disclosure must establish those connections.

### 2. Buyer's agent performance and potential negligence

The buyers question whether Darcy Knott and Trueblood Real Estate exercised reasonable care, diligence, and loyalty during the transaction.

Specific concerns include:

- **Square-footage investigation:** Darcy knew the buyers questioned conflicting measurements but relied on assessor data without obtaining the available plans or recommending an independent measurement before closing.
- **Inspection coordination:** Regina and Sam performed significant inspection scheduling and follow-up themselves. When an HVAC appointment was reportedly refused by the seller, the buyers repeatedly sought assistance resolving the problem.
- **Inspection advice:** The buyers specifically requested an evaluation of HVAC condition, defects, remaining useful life, and repair or replacement costs. The initial Chapman assessment did not satisfy those requests. The buyers question whether Darcy adequately addressed that distinction or helped secure complete evaluations before closing.
- **Contract risk explanations:** Darcy advised that hiring an attorney for the general transaction was usually unnecessary, although she recommended legal review of the easement. The buyers question whether she adequately explained the legal and practical consequences of as-is language, waived appraisal protections, incomplete inspections, and contractual acknowledgments concerning square footage.
- **Offer and easement communications:** The buyers believe certain language communicated to the sellers changed or expanded their intended position concerning a potential post-closing easement. They question whether their approved instructions were accurately conveyed.
- **Transaction management:** The buyers report that Darcy did not personally visit the property before closing and was frequently traveling or on vacation during negotiations and inspections. The correspondence confirms periods of travel but also documents substantial remote communication.
- **Protection of buyer interests:** The buyers question whether Darcy should have advised postponing closing, obtaining further information, or declining to proceed until important uncertainties had been addressed.

These are allegations requiring evaluation against the actual agency agreement, transaction documents, communications, applicable professional standards, and resulting damages.

### 3. Easement negotiations and their effect on the transaction

The sellers initially requested a recreational easement involving approximately one acre and access to the lake.

The negotiations consumed substantial time and legal attention. The buyers attempted to accommodate the request while protecting their privacy and property rights.

On August 5, the buyers' attorney discovered that the sellers intended to use substantially more of the lake than the buyers understood had been agreed upon.

The buyers subsequently abandoned the easement proposal and offered alternative purchase terms.

The buyers believe these negotiations diverted attention and resources from property-condition concerns and contributed to pressure to complete the transaction.

However, the correspondence also demonstrates that the buyers actively participated in proposing easement alternatives, obtained legal advice, and ultimately elected an as-is purchase structure. Those facts must be considered when evaluating causation and responsibility.

### 4. Effect of the as-is agreement

The final amendment increased the purchase price to $2,525,000, eliminated the easement, and provided that the property would be received as-is without repairs or repair costs.

The buyers are concerned that the agreement, together with other contractual acknowledgments or releases, may limit claims against the sellers and brokerage.

The principal dispute is whether those provisions bar claims involving affirmative misrepresentation, fraudulent concealment, or independently actionable professional negligence.

The buyers' explicit knowledge of some inspection concerns before closing, and their own proposal of an as-is option, are material adverse facts that must be included in any legal assessment.
