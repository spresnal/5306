## Disputed issues and allegations

**Property:** 5306 State Road 144, Greenwood, Indiana 46143  
**Purchase price:** $2,525,000  
**Closing date:** August 7, 2026

The buyers, Sam and Regina Presnal, believe the sellers, Derrick and Sarah Hasenour, may have misrepresented or concealed material information about the property. They also question whether their buyer's agent, Darcy Knott of Trueblood Real Estate, fulfilled her professional obligations.

**Allegations concerning the sellers:**

- **Square footage:** The property was marketed as 8,555 square feet despite historical listing information, architectural plans, and a subsequent appraisal suggesting significantly less finished living space. The sellers may have possessed information contradicting the advertised figure.
- **Bat infestation:** The sellers represented that a September 2024 insurance claim of approximately $25,000 resulted in comprehensive bat exclusion, removal of contaminated insulation and waste, disinfection, and replacement insulation. Post-closing findings suggest this work may not have been completed as represented. Active bat activity was also discovered.
- **Water intrusion:** The sellers acknowledged previous moisture issues and drainage repairs but represented that the problems were resolved. Post-closing discoveries raise questions about recurring moisture problems and the sellers' prior knowledge.
- **Fireplaces:** Both fireplaces were subsequently found to have substantial defects. The main-floor fireplace has reportedly cracked flue tiles and an undersized flue. The basement fireplace exhibits rust and apparent overheating damage. The buyers report that the seller disclosure indicated no defects.
- **Inspection interference:** Derrick allegedly refused access to a separately scheduled HVAC technician and made other inspections unnecessarily difficult. The buyers were unable to complete every desired specialized evaluation before closing.
- **Other property conditions:** Additional concerns include concealed or longstanding plumbing leaks, animal entry points, appliance condition, and potentially undisclosed prior repairs.

**Allegations concerning the buyer's agent:**

- **Failure to investigate square footage:** Regina repeatedly requested architectural plans and expressly questioned conflicting square-footage figures. Darcy relied on county assessor figures without obtaining independent verification or clearly recommending a professional measurement.
- **Inadequate inspection coordination:** Regina personally handled substantial inspection scheduling. Darcy was aware of seller resistance and incomplete evaluations, but the transaction proceeded without all requested assessments.
- **Unauthorized negotiation changes:** Darcy added wording to at least one buyer-approved easement proposal before forwarding it to the sellers, potentially altering its meaning.
- **Insufficient contractual guidance:** Darcy indicated that hiring an attorney for a transaction was generally unnecessary, although she recommended legal assistance for the easement. The buyers question whether she adequately explained contractual risks, including the as-is amendment and square-footage disclaimers.
- **Representation concerns:** Darcy was traveling during substantial portions of the transaction and reportedly did not physically visit the property before closing. The buyers question whether reasonable professional care and brokerage supervision were exercised.

The buyers acknowledge that they signed an as-is amendment, that the original contract contains potentially significant disclaimers or releases, and that some inspection concerns were known before closing.

The central allegations concern affirmative misrepresentations, potentially concealed information, professional judgment, and whether reasonable opportunities to investigate were compromised.
